\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Safety--accuracy trade-off.} Replacing symmetric MSE with \texttt{AsymmetricRULLoss} illustrates a fundamental trade-off for industrial predictive maintenance. By penalizing late predictions $5\times$ more heavily than early ones, the model shifts its predictions conservatively downward, cutting late-prediction rates roughly in half across every tested configuration and sharply curbing tail-risk variance in decentralized NASA scores (e.g., FD002 FedAvg std drops from \AsymFdTwoFedMseNasaStd{} to \AsymFdTwoFedAsyNasaStd{}). In safety-critical turbomachinery, fewer unpredicted failures outweigh slightly earlier scheduled maintenance; however, on heterogeneous multi-regime data (FD002) this conservatism raises symmetric RMSE by $10.8\%$--$27.9\%$ and can increase the centralized NASA score when early-prediction errors grow large. Operators can tune the $(\alpha_{\text{late}}, \alpha_{\text{early}})$ ratio to match asset criticality and false-alarm budgets.

The comparisons are seed-paired and use a common preprocessing path, but five seeds provide limited statistical resolution; the exact two-sided sign-flip test cannot return $p<0.05$ with this sample size and assumes sign-exchangeability of paired differences under the null. The reported tests are exploratory and no multiple-comparison correction is applied. The asymmetric NASA score is unbounded and can be dominated by a small number of large errors, so RMSE and score are reported together.

The global min--max scaler uses all training engines and therefore represents a centralized preprocessing step. Model updates are sent without TLS, secure aggregation, or differential privacy. Keeping telemetry local is a data-flow property, not a guarantee against inference from updates. The benchmark also uses simulated turbofan trajectories, two clients, fixed training schedules, no validation-based hyperparameter selection, and one compact model family plus a small architecture ablation. Results do not establish performance on industrial telemetry or production edge networks. Per-round payload bytes count model tensors and not transport framing or retransmissions.
