\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Safety--accuracy trade-off.} In the controlled FD001/FD002 comparison, replacing symmetric MSE with quadratic \texttt{AsymmetricRULLoss} shifts predictions conservatively downward and reduces late-prediction rates by \AsymLateReductionMin{}\%--\AsymLateReductionMax{}\%. It also lowers decentralized NASA-score variance (e.g., FD002 FedAvg standard deviation drops from \AsymFdTwoFedMseNasaStd{} to \AsymFdTwoFedAsyNasaStd{}). This may be desirable when RUL overestimation is costlier than early maintenance; however, on FD002 the same conservatism raises symmetric RMSE by \AsymFdTwoRmseIncreaseMin{}\%--\AsymFdTwoRmseIncreaseMax{}\% and increases the centralized NASA score. The paired-suite capped exponential ablation is a separate experiment and does not establish that the two asymmetric objectives are interchangeable. Operators would need to tune and validate the $(\alpha_{\text{late}}, \alpha_{\text{early}})$ ratio against asset-specific costs.

The comparisons are seed-paired and use a common preprocessing path, but five seeds provide limited statistical resolution; the exact two-sided sign-flip test cannot return $p<0.05$ with this sample size and assumes sign-exchangeability of paired differences under the null. The reported tests are exploratory and no multiple-comparison correction is applied. The asymmetric NASA score is unbounded and can be dominated by a small number of large errors, so RMSE and score are reported together.

The global min--max scaler uses all training engines and therefore represents a centralized preprocessing step. Model updates are sent without TLS, secure aggregation, or differential privacy. Keeping telemetry local is a data-flow property, not a guarantee against inference from updates. The benchmark also uses simulated turbofan trajectories, two clients, fixed training schedules, no validation-based hyperparameter selection, and one compact model family plus a small architecture ablation. Results do not establish performance on industrial telemetry or production edge networks. Per-round payload bytes count model tensors and not transport framing or retransmissions.

\textbf{Scope of the diagnostics evidence.} C-MAPSS provides unit-level RUL only and no per-unit component labels, so the diagnostics results are consistency and separability checks, not component-diagnosis accuracy. The shares in~\eqref{eq:attr} explain what moves this model's estimate relative to a healthy baseline; they are not a causal fault localization. The explained models are isolated per-node regressors trained for \DiagEpochs{} epochs, not the federated models of Tables~\ref{tab:main}--\ref{tab:asymmetric}, and the healthy baseline is fitted on all training engines of each subset.

\textbf{Core versus $\phi$/Turbine.} The robust single-fault result is the split between the core and the Fan/LPC side. The split \emph{within} the core depends on the channel map: $\phi$ (s12) is the ratio of fuel flow to the HPC outlet pressure Ps30, and we assign it to the Turbine group. On FD001 the model relies heavily on $\phi$, so most low-RUL windows are labeled Turbine-primary even though the simulated fault is in the HPC. Moving $\phi$ to the HPC group would change the HPC-only rate, but not the core rate. The HPC rate should therefore be read together with this mapping. The low HPC rate of plain saliency also shows that the baseline matters: $|\partial\hat y/\partial x|$ describes model sensitivity at the current input, whereas $(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b})\odot\nabla$ weights it by the engine's deviation from its own healthy state.

\textbf{Drift direction versus share bars.} On FD003/FD004, the fan-mode candidate drifts more than the HPC-like cluster in \emph{every} subsystem in mean $|z|$, including HPC (FD003: \DiagFdThreeCandAbsZHpc{} vs.\ \DiagFdThreeHpcAbsZHpc{}), and its attribution subsystem shares differ by only a few points. A subsystem share bar therefore does not by itself tell the two modes apart. The separation comes from the sign pattern of the sensor drift: P30, $\phi$ and the coolant bleeds reverse direction and bypass ratio falls. Shares are normalized magnitudes, so they discard this sign. On the six-regime FD004, attribution-only clusters are also not seed-stable and agree only weakly with the drift clusters. Signed drift profiles, alone or combined with attribution, are the better input for separating modes on such data.

\textbf{Edge cost evidence.} All diagnostics latency was measured on one thread of the Apple Silicon \texttt{\DiagMachine{}} development host, not on a Raspberry Pi~4. Attribution needs a backward pass and drift is recomputed in pandas, so the full path costs \DiagLatRatioMin{}--\DiagLatRatioMax{}$\times$ inference, while its absolute value is at most \DiagLatFullMax{}~ms per window on that host. A C-MAPSS window advances once per engine cycle. Whether the cost is acceptable on Pi-class hardware is an inference from these host figures and that cadence, not a measured result. The FD001 timing may also have overlapped a concurrent dashboard-data generation run. The socket audit covers only the diagnostics code path; it does not change the privacy scope of Section~\ref{sec:arch}.
