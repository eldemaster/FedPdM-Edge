\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
The ordering local-only $<$ federated $<$ centralised on FD002 matches what one expects from heterogeneity: each client sees a biased slice of degradation behaviour, and averaging recovers part of the pooled information. On FD001 the two clients are statistically alike, so there is little for FedAvg to correct, and the near-parity with centralised training is the expected outcome rather than a finding in itself.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) Only two clients; scaling behaviour is untested. (ii) Only 5 rounds, with RMSE still decreasing on FD002, so the federated result may not be converged and the gap to centralised may shrink. (iii) The federated test numbers are evaluated on the test set every round with no separate validation set; no model selection is done on it, but \todo{confirm}. (iv) Five seeds give wide NASA-score deviations on FD002. (v) Min--max scaling uses global statistics computed centrally (\texttt{compute\_global\_scale.py}), which is a privacy leak relative to a fully federated pipeline. (vi) Client \texttt{evaluate()} returns a placeholder loss, so distributed loss is uninformative (audit item P20). (vii) C-MAPSS is simulated data; transfer to real plants is untested. (viii) FD002 partition method requires confirmation (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). \todo{FedProx, FD004, edge-cost measurements, differential privacy / secure aggregation}
