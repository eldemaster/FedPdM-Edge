\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
The ordering local-only $<$ federated $<$ centralised on FD002 matches what one expects from heterogeneity: each client sees a biased slice of degradation behaviour, and averaging recovers part of the pooled information. On FD001 the two clients are statistically alike, so there is little for FedAvg to correct, and the near-parity with centralised training is the expected outcome rather than a finding in itself.

\textbf{Safety--accuracy trade-off.} Replacing symmetric MSE with \texttt{AsymmetricRULLoss} illustrates a fundamental trade-off for industrial predictive maintenance. By penalising late predictions $5\times$ more heavily than early ones, the model shifts its predictions conservatively downward, cutting late-prediction rates roughly in half across every tested configuration and sharply curbing tail-risk variance in decentralized NASA scores (e.g., FD002 FedAvg std drops from \AsymFdTwoFedMseNasaStd{} to \AsymFdTwoFedAsyNasaStd{}). In safety-critical turbomachinery, fewer unpredicted failures outweigh slightly earlier scheduled maintenance; however, on heterogeneous multi-regime data (FD002) this conservatism raises symmetric RMSE by $10.8\%$--$27.9\%$ and can increase the centralised NASA score when early-prediction errors grow large. Operators can tune the $(\alpha_{\text{late}}, \alpha_{\text{early}})$ ratio to match asset criticality and false-alarm budgets.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) Only two clients; scaling behaviour is untested. (ii) Only 5 rounds, with RMSE still decreasing on FD002, so the federated result may not be converged and the gap to centralised may shrink. (iii) The federated test numbers are evaluated on the test set every round with no separate validation set; no model selection is done on it, but \todo{confirm}. (iv) Five seeds give wide NASA-score deviations on FD002 under MSE, though \texttt{AsymmetricRULLoss} substantially narrows decentralized variance. (v) Min--max scaling uses global statistics computed centrally (\texttt{compute\_global\_scale.py}), which is a privacy leak relative to a fully federated pipeline. (vi) Client \texttt{evaluate()} returns a placeholder loss, so distributed loss is uninformative (audit item P20). (vii) C-MAPSS is simulated data; transfer to real plants is untested. (viii) FD002 partition method requires confirmation (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). \todo{FedProx, FD004, edge-cost measurements, differential privacy / secure aggregation}
