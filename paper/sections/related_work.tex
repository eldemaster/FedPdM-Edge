\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Centralised RUL prediction on C-MAPSS}
The C-MAPSS benchmark and the PHM08 evaluation conventions originate in NASA prognostics work~\cite{saxena2008damage,saxena2008metrics}. Early deep baselines include a convolutional regressor evaluated on all four subsets by Li et al.~\cite{li2018cnn}; later work combines recurrent, convolutional, and attention-based sequence models. In a later comparative table, CNN-LSTM is reported at RMSE 14.40, 27.36, 14.32, and 26.69 with scores 290, 9869, 316, and 6594; a dual-task LSTM reports RMSE 12.29, 17.87, 14.34, and 21.81 (score not reported)~\cite{liu2025moeformer}. For scale, the CNN baseline in that table is reported at RMSE 18.45, 30.29, 19.82, and 29.16 on FD001--FD004, respectively~\cite{liu2025moeformer}. A 2024 self-attention TCN reports RMSE 11.50, 16.45, 11.62, and 15.47, with NASA scores 225.32, 1136.27, 259.79, and 1365.40~\cite{wang2024adamsamtcn}. MoEFormer, a condition-specialised Transformer architecture, reports RMSE 15.13, 13.71, 13.88, and 14.47 and scores 395.60, 792.64, 290.67, and 1008.25~\cite{liu2025moeformer}.

These published values establish context, not a direct leaderboard comparison: preprocessing, sensor selection, RUL caps, validation, seeds, and the precise test protocol vary across papers. Our paired suite uses a 30-cycle input, a 125-cycle cap, and one terminal window per official test engine, and does not claim state-of-the-art accuracy.

\subsection{Federated learning}
FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} averages locally trained weights, weighted by client sample counts. Non-IID data degrades it~\cite{zhao2018noniid}; FedProx adds a proximal term to limit client drift~\cite{li2020fedprox}. Adaptive federated optimizers such as FedAdam apply server-side adaptive updates to aggregated client deltas~\cite{reddi2021adaptive}. Overviews of open problems are in~\cite{kairouz2021advances,li2020challenges}. The repository's earlier deployment demo uses Flower~\cite{beutel2020flower}; the reproducible comparison suite implements the aggregation loop locally to hold the baseline protocol fixed.

\subsection{Federated prognostics and heterogeneity}
Federated averaging (FedAvg) is the baseline used here~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}. RUL federation on C-MAPSS is not new: Rosero et al. studied decentralized RUL estimation for aircraft components~\cite{rosero2020federated}; S\"oderkvist Vermelin et al. compared FedAvg with personalized layers on C-MAPSS and a SiC MOSFET dataset~\cite{soderkvist2024collaborative}; and Chen et al. combined federated training with network pruning and model rebirth for compact RUL prediction~\cite{chen2023pruning}. Landau et al. studied an airline collaboration framework on N-CMAPSS, including decentralized validation and noise-robust aggregation~\cite{landau2025aircraftfl}. Most directly, the 2026 FedCMAPSS preprint defines five C-MAPSS federation tasks spanning IID, domain shift, lifespan-label skew, feature skew, and few-shot heterogeneity, and evaluates multiple FL optimizers and architectures~\cite{sorrenti2026fedcmapss}. It makes broad simulated benchmark claims that overlap with the algorithmic question in this paper.

Non-IID data can cause client drift under local updates. FedProx constrains local movement with a proximal term~\cite{li2020fedprox}; SCAFFOLD uses control variates~\cite{karimireddy2020scaffold}; and server-side adaptive methods such as FedAdam/FedYogi alter aggregation dynamics~\cite{reddi2021adaptive}. Personalization layers address client-specific optima, as directly studied for federated RUL~\cite{soderkvist2024collaborative}. Clustered FL instead groups clients with related objectives~\cite{sattler2021clustered}. These are candidate comparisons for our two-client regime, not methods implemented in the current code. The FD002 partition in the checked-in generator is ordered by engine lifespan, which induces label skew; it is not a partition by operating condition. Any claim about operating-condition-specific non-IID clients must wait for an explicit split and its saved assignment.

\subsection{Asymmetric risk and evaluation}
The PHM08/NASA score penalizes late RUL predictions more sharply than early predictions~\cite{saxena2008damage,saxena2008metrics}. Because the raw exponential score exhibits gradients that grow as $\exp(|d|/10)$ on large early-training errors, direct exponential optimization is numerically unstable. In this study we compare standard symmetric MSE against a smooth asymmetric quadratic surrogate (\texttt{AsymmetricRULLoss}, Section~\ref{sec:method}) under the same data and seed protocol. The reported NASA sum is sensitive to a small number of large late errors and test-set size, so it should accompany RMSE and the late-prediction rate.

\subsection{Privacy and edge deployment}
Keeping raw telemetry local reduces direct data centralisation but does not, by itself, provide a formal privacy guarantee. Model updates can reveal information; differential privacy requires a mechanism and an accounted privacy budget, while secure aggregation hides individual updates from the aggregator~\cite{abadi2016dpsgd,bonawitz2017secureagg}. A 2026 RUL-specific study applies differential privacy to federated RUL models, including C-MAPSS, and reports privacy--utility trade-offs~\cite{nikulins2026dpRul}. This repository does not implement differential privacy or secure aggregation. Source inspection also found no TLS credentials or secure-channel configuration in the Flower client/server calls; the README's mTLS and encrypted-weight claims are therefore unsupported for the checked-in experiment. Model parameters are sent over the ordinary Flower gRPC path. Furthermore, global scaling statistics are centrally computed and distributed, so the full preprocessing pipeline is not private.

Federated learning on Raspberry Pi-class and other constrained devices is evaluated in prior systems work for training time, memory, power, and communication overhead~\cite{gao2020iotedge,sebbio2023raspberrypi}; those measures are necessary to support an edge-feasibility claim. This study has physical Raspberry Pi 4 clients, but its checked-in results do not record training time, peak memory, energy, or measured network traffic. The model-size-based payload estimate in the method is analytical, not an observed communication measurement. We therefore describe the work as a physical deployment study, not yet a measured resource-efficiency study.

\subsection{Positioning and evaluation protocol}
The present contribution is a small physical testbed evaluation of a Flower/PyTorch CNN with two Raspberry Pi clients, an Ubuntu coordinator, local-only and centralised reference runs, five nominal seeds, and both RMSE and NASA score. It is an engineering evaluation rather than a new optimizer. Scientific claims are limited by two clients, five communication rounds, two C-MAPSS subsets, no measured edge costs, and unresolved FD002 split provenance for archived runs. A stronger comparison should fix unit-level partitions and preprocessing, train local/central/federated methods on the identical training units, report client-specific and global test metrics, use more seeds and paired uncertainty/significance analysis, and reserve validation data for any model or hyperparameter selection.
