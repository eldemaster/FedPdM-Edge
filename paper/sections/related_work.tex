\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Centralized RUL prediction on C-MAPSS}
The C-MAPSS benchmark and the PHM08 evaluation conventions originate in NASA prognostics work~\cite{saxena2008damage,saxena2008metrics}. Early deep baselines include a convolutional regressor evaluated on all four subsets by Li et al.~\cite{li2018cnn}; later work combines recurrent, convolutional, and attention-based sequence models. In a later comparative table, CNN-LSTM is reported at RMSE 14.40, 27.36, 14.32, and 26.69 with scores 290, 9869, 316, and 6594; a dual-task LSTM reports RMSE 12.29, 17.87, 14.34, and 21.81 (score not reported)~\cite{liu2025moeformer}. For scale, the CNN baseline in that table is reported at RMSE 18.45, 30.29, 19.82, and 29.16 on FD001--FD004, respectively~\cite{liu2025moeformer}. A 2024 self-attention TCN reports RMSE 11.50, 16.45, 11.62, and 15.47, with NASA scores 225.32, 1136.27, 259.79, and 1365.40~\cite{wang2024adamsamtcn}. MoEFormer, a condition-specialised Transformer architecture, reports RMSE 15.13, 13.71, 13.88, and 14.47 and scores 395.60, 792.64, 290.67, and 1008.25~\cite{liu2025moeformer}.

These published values establish context, not a direct leaderboard comparison: preprocessing, sensor selection, RUL caps, validation, seeds, and the precise test protocol vary across papers. Our paired suite uses a 30-cycle input, a 125-cycle cap, and one terminal window per official test engine, and does not claim state-of-the-art accuracy.

\subsection{Federated learning}
FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} averages locally trained weights, weighted by client sample counts. Non-IID data degrades it~\cite{zhao2018noniid}; FedProx adds a proximal term to limit client drift~\cite{li2020fedprox}. Adaptive federated optimizers such as FedAdam apply server-side adaptive updates to aggregated client deltas~\cite{reddi2021adaptive}. Overviews of open problems are in~\cite{kairouz2021advances,li2020challenges}. The repository's earlier deployment demo uses Flower~\cite{beutel2020flower}; the reproducible comparison suite implements the aggregation loop locally to hold the baseline protocol fixed.

\subsection{Federated prognostics and heterogeneity}
Federated averaging (FedAvg) is the baseline used here~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}. RUL federation on C-MAPSS is not new: Rosero et al. studied decentralized RUL estimation for aircraft components~\cite{rosero2020federated}; S\"oderkvist Vermelin et al. compared FedAvg with personalized layers on C-MAPSS and a SiC MOSFET dataset~\cite{soderkvist2024collaborative}; and Chen et al. combined federated training with network pruning and model rebirth for compact RUL prediction~\cite{chen2023pruning}. Landau et al. studied an airline collaboration framework on N-CMAPSS, including decentralized validation and noise-robust aggregation~\cite{landau2025aircraftfl}. Most directly, the 2026 FedCMAPSS preprint defines five C-MAPSS federation tasks spanning IID, domain shift, lifespan-label skew, feature skew, and few-shot heterogeneity, and evaluates multiple FL optimizers and architectures~\cite{sorrenti2026fedcmapss}. It makes broad simulated benchmark claims that overlap with the algorithmic question in this paper.

Non-IID data can cause client drift under local updates. FedProx constrains local movement with a proximal term~\cite{li2020fedprox}; SCAFFOLD uses control variates~\cite{karimireddy2020scaffold}; and server-side adaptive methods such as FedAdam/FedYogi alter aggregation dynamics~\cite{reddi2021adaptive}. Personalization layers address client-specific optima, as directly studied for federated RUL~\cite{soderkvist2024collaborative}. Clustered FL instead groups clients with related objectives~\cite{sattler2021clustered}. Our paired suite implements FedProx and FedAdam. Its regime partition assigns windows by endpoint operating condition, while its engine-count-imbalance partition preserves whole engines. Because a physical engine can contribute windows to both regime clients, the former tests operating-regime shift but does not represent independent sites.

\subsection{Asymmetric risk and evaluation}
The PHM08/NASA score penalizes late RUL predictions more sharply than early predictions~\cite{saxena2008damage,saxena2008metrics}. Direct exponential optimization can be numerically unstable because its gradients grow exponentially with error magnitude. The paired suite therefore caps the exponent in its score-aware ablation, while the separate FD001/FD002 comparison evaluates a smooth asymmetric quadratic surrogate (\texttt{AsymmetricRULLoss}, Section~\ref{sec:method}) against MSE. The reported NASA sum is sensitive to a small number of large errors and test-set size, so it should accompany RMSE and late-prediction rate.

\subsection{Privacy and edge deployment}
Keeping raw telemetry local reduces direct data centralization but does not, by itself, provide a formal privacy guarantee. Model updates can reveal information; differential privacy requires a mechanism and an accounted privacy budget, while secure aggregation hides individual updates from the aggregator~\cite{abadi2016dpsgd,bonawitz2017secureagg}. A 2026 RUL-specific study applies differential privacy to federated RUL models, including C-MAPSS, and reports privacy--utility trade-offs~\cite{nikulins2026dpRul}. This repository implements neither mechanism. The legacy Flower client/server calls also lack TLS configuration, while the paired suite simulates aggregation locally and does not measure transport. Furthermore, both protocols use centrally computed scaling statistics, so the preprocessing pipeline is not private.

Federated learning on Raspberry Pi-class and other constrained devices is evaluated in prior systems work for training time, memory, power, and communication overhead~\cite{gao2020iotedge,sebbio2023raspberrypi}; those measures are necessary to support an edge-feasibility claim. The paired suite includes a profiler for CPU training time, peak process RSS and tensor payload. Its committed artifact is a one-round Apple Silicon smoke run; no Raspberry Pi profile, energy measurement or network wire capture is available. We therefore make no resource-efficiency claim for Raspberry Pi hardware.

\subsection{Positioning and evaluation protocol}
The present contribution is an engineering evaluation rather than a new optimizer. The paired suite covers FD001--FD004, five matched seeds, global and client metrics, explicit IID/regime/engine-imbalance partitions, FedProx/FedAdam and architecture/loss ablations. The complementary quadratic-loss study covers FD001/FD002 under its own controlled five-seed protocol. Claims remain limited by two simulated clients, five communication rounds, fixed hyperparameters, centralized scaling, small seed counts and the lack of Raspberry Pi profile data. The two protocols are reported separately because their seeds, preprocessing and batch sizes differ.
